Part two

The four planned features

Upstream's TODO list, costed against the code as it stands. Sizing is honest effort for someone who already knows this codebase: S is an evening, M is a day or two, L is a project in its own right.

Half done

Settings: change strip type

The RGB/RGBW half is already shipped — that is the runtime W=0|1|2 switch we added. What upstream also meant is switching between WS2812 and APA102 without recompiling, and that part is still open.

It looks hard because FastLED.addLeds<CHIPSET, PIN>() is a template resolved at compile time. The trick is that you do not have to choose: register both controllers at boot and use CLEDController::setEnabled() to mute the one you are not using. Cost is a few hundred bytes of RAM for the second controller's state.

Snag: APA102 needs a clock pin, so the pin budget changes with the setting. Document GPIO 17 as reserved rather than reusing it elsewhere.
M

Digitised audio, with mixing

Right now a hardware timer toggles the internal DAC to make a square wave. The whole audio surface is five functions and five call sites, so the engine can be swapped without touching a line of game logic — that is the reason this is an M and not an L.

  • An I2S output stage feeding a mixer with, say, four voices; each voice is a pointer into a sample, a playback position and a gain.
  • sound(freq, vol) stays as the compatibility path so nothing breaks on day one, then individual effects move over to play(SFX_KILL) one at a time.
  • Lava hum and movement can finally overlap, which is exactly what upstream wanted.
Snag: the real constraint is flash, not code. There are roughly 470 KB free — about 21 seconds at 22 kHz 8-bit mono. Plenty for effects, nowhere near enough for music. Also note SoundData.h already holds 14 KB of WAV data that nothing includes; it has been waiting for this rewrite since the Arduino version.
M

A web interface that behaves like an app

The current one is a hand-rolled HTTP server: it reads the socket byte by byte, looks for GET /?, then finds the value by searching for the next = and the following H — on the assumption that HTTP/ comes right after. Every setting is its own form, and every change reloads the whole page.

  • Replace the parser with WebServer or ESPAsyncWebServer. This alone removes a genuinely brittle piece of code.
  • Serve one small page plus a /api/settings endpoint in JSON: GET to read, POST to write. Live values without a reload.
  • Keep /metrics exactly as it is — the Prometheus endpoint is already there and already useful.
  • Add mDNS while you are in there. twang.local beats memorising 192.168.4.1.
Snag: serve the page from PROGMEM, not SPIFFS, so a firmware flash can never leave the UI and the firmware out of step.
L

Two players over a wireless link

The biggest of the four, and the one whose difficulty is design rather than code. Upstream marked it TBD for good reason: nobody has decided what two-player actually is here.

  • Transport: ESP-NOW, not WiFi. Sub-10 ms, no access point, no association. The catch is that ESP-NOW and a running SoftAP must share one radio and one channel, so the settings AP has to yield during a match.
  • Topology: one device owns the game state, the other sends its joystick reading and receives what to draw. Never let both simulate — that way lies desync.
  • What the game becomes: two players entering the same strip from opposite ends is the version that needs no new hardware and reads instantly to a spectator.
Worth knowing: the code already supports a second MPU6050 at 0x69, meant for handheld use. Put one at each end of a single strip and you get local two-player with no radio at all — a far cheaper way to find out whether the mode is even fun before building the wireless version.
Part three

What I would add

Ordered by leverage rather than by size. The first two are cheap and pay off immediately; the third changes what the project can become.

S

Over-the-air updates

WiFi is already up at boot. Adding ArduinoOTA is a handful of lines and ends the routine of opening the enclosure to reach the USB port. Once the thing is screwed shut and the spring is mounted, this stops being a convenience and becomes the difference between tweaking a level and not bothering.

S

Stop writing EEPROM on every death

Play statistics are committed to flash at every game over. During one unattended session the counter reached 58 — that is 58 flash erase cycles from nobody playing. Write on a timer, or only when the values actually changed and the device has been idle for a while.

M

Levels as data, not as a switch statement

This is the one I would actually push for. Every level is already nothing but a short list of spawn calls — look at the maps above, that is the entire content of the game. Turn that switch into an array of records and three things fall out at once:

  • Levels become editable over the web UI, no toolchain, no reflash.
  • Level sets can be stored, swapped and shared as a small JSON file.
  • Two-player and any future mode stop needing their own copy of the level logic.
Sequencing note: do this before the two-player work, not after. Retrofitting a data model underneath a finished multiplayer implementation is how weekend projects die.

Lamp mode

The screensavers are already there — Fire2012, sinelon, juggle. Expose them as a deliberate ambient mode with its own brightness, and the thing earns its shelf space when nobody is playing.

High scores with initials

A single high score is stored today. A five-entry table, entered by tilting to pick a letter and twanging to confirm, costs almost nothing and is the natural use of the controller you already have.

Practice / level select

The serial command V= already jumps to a level. Surfacing it in the web UI turns a debug hook into a feature, particularly while you are tuning new levels.

Haptics on hit

A small vibration motor in the knob, driven from a spare GPIO. On a controller you grip, the hit landing in your hand does more for feel than any sound effect.

Attack cooldown & combos

Attacks are currently free. A short cooldown plus a score multiplier for consecutive kills would give the twang a cost and the scoring some texture.

Difficulty that adapts

Spawner rates and enemy speeds are plain numbers in each level. Scaling them by recent performance would keep the twentieth playthrough interesting without new content.

Part five · updated 2026-11-07

Wiring: one cable instead of two

Power no longer comes in on the ESP32's micro-USB, and the strip no longer has a cable of its own. Both run over one 9-conductor serial cable, which carries only strip power (5 V, GND) and the two strip signals. The MPU6050 and the sound module stay on the controller side. USB is now only for flashing and the serial console. Firmware is built for USE_SK9822: clocked strip, data on GPIO 16, clock on GPIO 17.

Corrected 2026-11-07
The first wiring had the strip's red and white swapped: red (+5 V) on GND, white on 5 V. Now red is on terminal 1 (5 V) and white on terminal 2 (GND). If a strip is ever re-wired, go by the labels printed on the strip PCB at the input end (5V / GND / DI / CI), not by the wire colours.

Colours lie in this cable

Black (1) and red (3) are both +5 V; brown and orange are GND. Nothing here follows “red plus, black minus”. Go by terminal number, never by colour.

Share the current

5 V and GND each have two conductors (1+3, 2+4), but the strip only lands on 1 and 2. Bridge 3→1 and 4→2 at the far end too. With a 4.2 A budget (M=4200), a single thin conductor is not enough. For long runs, feed strip power directly at the far end and keep only a common GND in the cable.

USB and VIN together

The ESP32 now runs from VIN. Only plug USB in while the external 5 V is on if the module has a diode between USB 5 V and VIN. Otherwise the two supplies feed into each other.

Spare conductors

Yellow (5), purple (8) and grey (9) are in the cable but connected at neither end. Free for later, for example a button or a second sensor at the far end.

Shield at one end only

Connect the bare drain wire to GND at the board end only. Grounded at both ends it forms a loop parallel to the GND conductors.

Volume in software

The PAM8403's gain is fixed at ~24 dB, so its pot sits near minimum. Set it low once and adjust with S= (serial or web UI).
